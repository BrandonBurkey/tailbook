# Objection Handling

**How to use this file:** acknowledge the objection, ask one question, reframe it with the value equation, then offer a low-risk next step. Never argue, and never overclaim. If the objection is true for this person, say so and let them go. In a community this small, our reputation is the distribution channel (decision rules §5).

**Value equation:** Value = (Dream outcome × Perceived likelihood) ÷ (Time delay × Effort and sacrifice). Every response below raises the top half or shrinks the bottom half. None of them claims the app certifies airworthiness.

**Log every objection** in `crm-pipeline.csv` (`notes`) and, for owners, in the tracker. The decision rules count "my shop handles it" and privacy above 30% as negative tiebreakers, so they have to be recorded honestly.

---

### 1. "I use a spreadsheet. It works fine."
- **Acknowledge:** "A good spreadsheet beats nothing, and plenty of MooneySpace owners run one."
- **Ask:** "When did you last update it, and does it get the calendar-month end date right? For example, is an ELT checked on May 3 due by the end of May next year?"
- **Reframe:** the spreadsheet only works if you open it. TailBook's job is the part a spreadsheet can't do:
  - it warns you 60, 30 and 7 days out
  - it projects hour-based items from how you actually fly
  - it attaches the logbook page as proof
  - your partner sees the same board
- **Effort:** setup is about 5 minutes, and there's a 14-day free trial to compare it against the spreadsheet.
- **If they still say no:** "Fair enough. Here's the free 24-month cheat sheet so your spreadsheet catches those items too."

### 2. "ForeFlight (or Garmin) will just do this."
- **Acknowledge:** "They might. They're excellent at what they do, and I'd never ask you to drop them."
- **Reframe:** their focus is the flight, the pilot and the logbook. TailBook focuses on the **aircraft**: owner maintenance due dates, recurring ADs, squawks, scans and co-owner sharing. We stay out of their lane on purpose (no pilot logbook or currency tracking).
- **Risk reversal:** if a big player ships this and it's better, you can cancel. It's an annual subscription with no lock-in, and you can export your data as PDF. Meanwhile, this season's annual still needs a punch list.

### 3. "My A&P tracks all that for me."
- **Acknowledge:** "That's great. A good IA is the best asset an owner has."
- **Ask:** "Does your shop call you before the transponder check or the ELT battery comes due, or do you find out at the annual?"
- **Reframe:** legally, the **owner or operator** is responsible for keeping the aircraft maintained (91.403). The shop sees the plane once a year, and you fly it the other 364 days. TailBook makes you a better customer for your shop, because you arrive with a work order and squawks. It never second-guesses the IA, who still determines airworthiness.
- **Next step:** "Ask your IA to look at a sample Annual Work Order. If they think it's useless, I want to hear why." (This also turns the IA into a lead for the shop channel.)

### 4. "$199 a year is a lot for an app."
- **Ask:** "Compared with what?"
- **Reframe with anchors:**
  - $199 is **under 1% of a typical year's operating cost** ($15k–$35k)
  - it's about **one hour of shop labor**
  - it's well under a managed service (SavvyMx is about $750/yr, Bluetail about $1,095/yr for a piston)
  - one avoided surprise at the annual, or one 24-month item caught before it lapses, pays for years
- **Partnerships:** it's per aircraft with unlimited co-owners, so three partners pay **under $70 each**.
- **Founding offer:** $149/yr for as long as you keep the subscription, held with a $49 deposit that's **fully refundable any time before launch**. Then a 14-day trial, and the Find-It Guarantee in your first 30 days.
- **If budget really is the issue:** don't push. The free Snapshot and cheat sheet are still theirs.

### 5. "What if you shut down?"
- **Honest answer:** "That's a real risk with any small company, so here's what protects you."
  - Your data lives **on your device and in your own iCloud**, not on our servers, so it doesn't disappear if we do.
  - You can export PDFs any time.
  - Your scans are copies; keep your originals (our Terms say so).
  - Deposits are refunded automatically if we haven't shipped by **2027-06-30**, or if we decide not to build it.
- **Reframe:** the risk you're carrying today is a spreadsheet only you understand and logbooks in one box. TailBook reduces that risk even in the worst case.

### 6. "I'm not putting my logbooks or aircraft data in an app" (privacy)
- **Acknowledge:** "Good instinct. Your records are worth real money."
- **Facts:**
  - **No account and no TailBook servers.** Data is stored on your device and in your own iCloud (CloudKit).
  - Scanning and OCR run **on the device**.
  - We don't sell data or share it for advertising.
  - You decide who you share with (co-owners, your shop).
  - For the Snapshot, photos are used only to prepare it and are deleted within 90 days.
- **Effort reducer:** you don't have to scan anything to get value. Eight dates and your tach reading are enough for the board.

### 7. "Does it automatically know which ADs apply to my airplane?"
- **Honest answer:** "No, not in v1. You or your IA enter the recurring ADs, and TailBook tracks them with whichever-first logic, method-of-compliance notes and a link to the logbook page."
- **Reframe:** determining which ADs apply is your IA's job, and we won't pretend to do it (see our disclaimer). What owners actually lose track of is *when* the next recurring compliance is due, and that part we handle.
- **Future:** automatic AD matching from FAA data is a v2 candidate. We don't promise it.

### 8. "Is this FAA-approved? Can I show it to a ramp inspector or an insurer?"
- **Honest answer:** "No. It's an organizational tool, not an FAA-approved record. Your logbooks remain the official maintenance records under 91.417."
- **Reframe:** its value is that you're never surprised, and that you can hand your IA, insurer or buyer a clean summary that points to the real entries.

### 9. "My partners or club members won't use it."
- **Ask:** "What would they actually have to do?"
- **Reframe:** one tap on a home-screen widget logs tach after a flight. There's no login; they accept an iCloud share. Even if only one person maintains the board, every member can **see** what's due. For large clubs, members can get the exported Status PDF.
- **Proof:** we measure it. The target is 70%+ of aircraft logging tach at least once a month (E5). If clubs don't hit it, we'll know and fix it.

### 10. "Let me wait until it's actually launched."
- **Acknowledge:** "Totally reasonable."
- **Reframe the urgency honestly:**
  - Founding pricing is real scarcity: **250 aircraft**, then $199.
  - The $49 deposit is **refundable any time before launch**, so waiting doesn't lower your risk. It only loses the price.
  - Partners and depositors get TestFlight first.
- **No fake deadlines.** If they still want to wait: "I'll email you when it ships."

### 11. "Our club (or association) doesn't endorse products."
- **Acknowledge:** "Understood, and we don't want an endorsement."
- **Reframe:** the offer is **content plus a member discount**, like the vendor discounts many clubs already list. The article is IA-reviewed and non-promotional, with a one-line bio. We never use your name or logo without written approval, and your technical committee can review the template.
- **Smallest yes:** run the article, or a 400-word sidebar.

### 12. "We already use Flight Circle, Flight Schedule Pro or SavvyMx."
- **Acknowledge:** "Keep them. They do scheduling, billing or expert-managed maintenance well."
- **Reframe:** TailBook is the **owner-side** board in your pocket: FAR-cited dates, widget tach logging, scans and PDFs, for $199/aircraft. Schools use it for leaseback owners. Clubs use it so every member sees status without a login. SavvyMx clients use it as the reminder layer between Savvy's reviews.
- **Honesty:** if their current tool already covers everything, say so and walk away.

### 13. "What if TailBook gets a date wrong?"
- **Honest answer:**
  - The interval engine is deterministic and covered by tests built from real Snapshots.
  - An IA reviews the rules.
  - Every date shows its regulation, so you or your IA can check it.
  - It still can contain errors, and we say so. Always verify against the logbooks with your A&P/IA.
- **Risk reversal (the Error Promise):** "If a calculation error in TailBook (not in the data you entered) showed an item as current when it was overdue, we'll make it right. We'll help with an Apple refund request and give you a free year." It's a make-good, not a promise to cover consequential losses.

### 14. "I only fly 30 hours a year. It's not worth it."
- **Reframe:** low-time owners are *more* exposed to calendar items, not less. The annual, the 24-month transponder and pitot-static checks, the ELT and registration all come due whether you fly or not, and with fewer flights you have fewer moments that remind you. Hour-based items get projected from your actual pace.
- **Value per dollar:** if you're spending $15k a year to keep an airplane you fly 30 hours, $199 protects the whole investment.
- **If they're selling soon:** use the broker angle. A clean Status Report helps at sale.

### 15. "Monthly is fine. Why should I pay annually?"
- **Facts:** annual is $199, monthly is $24.99 (about $300/yr), so annual saves about a third. Founding pricing ($149) is annual only.
- **Reframe:** due items run on 12- and 24-month cycles, so the value builds over the full year.
- **If they want monthly:** fine. "Start monthly, and switch when you see your first alert land." Keep monthly as the downsell, not the default.
